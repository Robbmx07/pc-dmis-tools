PC-DMIS approach vectors explained (IJK without the headache)

More CMM programs go wrong from a flipped vector than from almost anything else. The math is simple. The confusion is almost always about what the vector is relative to.

What I, J and K are

I, J and K are the vector's components along X, Y and Z. Each one is a direction cosine: I is the cosine of the angle between the vector and the X axis, J to Y, and K to Z. Together they always form a unit vector, with a length of 1. IJK belongs to the fixed 3D coordinate system, not to the 2D workplane you happen to be in.

The vector is a surface normal

For planes, circles and cylinders, IJK is the direction perpendicular to the surface. It is not a description of tilt. When a plane tilts, its normal leans with it and stays a unit vector.

Part to probe: getting the sign right

The vector points from the surface toward the side the probe approached from, which is the opposite of the probe's travel. Probe moving down (−Z) onto a top face gives a hit vector of 0,0,1.

Two habits that prevent most vector problems

For features that aren't square to an axis, don't hand-calculate direction cosines. Square the part up with a temporary alignment, build the feature with a simple ±1 vector, and let the alignment carry the real orientation. And to reverse a vector, use the dialog's Flip Vector button instead of typing a minus sign, so the magnitude stays correct.

If a feature that used to probe cleanly starts crashing or reporting backwards after a CAD import, suspect the model's normals: re-pick the correct face in Auto Feature mode before changing anything else.
