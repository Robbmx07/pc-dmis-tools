3-2-1 alignment in PC-DMIS, step by step

An alignment's job is to lock all six degrees of freedom, three translations and three rotations, so every number on the report means what you think it means. A program can report everything in tolerance and still be wrong if the alignment leaves something free.

The rule: level, rotate, then origin

Level fixes which way is up. Rotate fixes the spin about that axis. Origin fixes the XYZ zero. Do them in that order. Translating before the part is fully leveled and rotated moves your origin out from under you.

Build it in the Alignment dialog

PC-DMIS rejects multi-line alignment text pasted into the Edit window, so build it with the dialog:

  1. Open the Alignment dialog with Ctrl+Alt+A (or Insert | Alignment | New).
  2. Select the datum A plane and click Level (Z+).
  3. Select the datum B line and click Rotate (X+ about Z+).
  4. Select the datum C feature, check X and Y, and click Origin. Then select datum A, check Z, and click Origin.
  5. Name it after the datum frame, for example ALN_ABC, and click OK.

When you're done, the Edit window shows ALIGNMENT/LEVEL, ALIGNMENT/ROTATE,XPLUS,TO,…,ABOUT,ZPLUS and three ALIGNMENT/TRANS lines between ALIGNMENT/START and ALIGNMENT/END.

Pitfalls that quietly corrupt results

  • Leaving a degree of freedom unlocked in the alignment you measure from.
  • Chaining alignments off each other instead of always recalling from the start.
  • Using variables inside alignment commands.
  • Naming alignments A1, A2. Name them after the datum frame so a reviewer can trace them to the print.
